## 1. Barnfield (2019/2020)

**Referência.** Barnfield, Matthew. 2020. "Think Twice before Jumping on the Bandwagon: Clarifying Concepts in Research on the Bandwagon Effect." *Political Studies Review* 18 (4): 553–574. doi:10.1177/1478929919870691. No Crossref, a publicação online é de 06/09/2019 e a impressa, de novembro de 2020.

**Acesso.** Na SAGE, o acesso é fechado (OpenAlex: `oa_status` closed). A versão aceita está no QMRO (`qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/handle/123456789/60342`), com o arquivo "Accepted version (57.85Kb)" e a nota "© 2019, © SAGE Publications". O QMRO respondeu a este agente com um desafio JavaScript antirrobô, que não foi contornado. O registro e o arquivo .docx da versão aceita foram lidos nas cópias do Internet Archive (capturas de 15/09/2024). Não houve PDF. A versão aceita não tem paginação, então cito a seção. Ela pode diferir da versão publicada, e o apêndice citado no texto não está no arquivo.

**Tipo de revisão.** É uma revisão conceitual, não sistemática nem meta-analítica. Na Introdução, o autor escreve: "It is worth noting that the aim of this paper is primarily conceptual and not to provide a meta-analysis or a systematic review of the evidence of bandwagon effects. As such, I do not attempt to quantify such findings or comment extensively on methodological issues."

**Número de estudos e seleção.**

- Introdução: "Based on a review of 65 peer-reviewed journal articles, I define the bandwagon effect as [...]".
- Seção "A typology of bandwagon effects": "65 journal articles are classified here, selected by consulting a previous review, for older literature (Irwin and Van Holsteyn 2000), and a Web of Science search with snowball sampling, for more recent studies. An Appendix gives more details on how these articles were selected and provides a more precise coding of each article (Table 4)."
- **Período:** o artigo não o declara. As obras classificadas na Tabela 2 vão de 1940 (Cook and Welch; Gallup and Rae) a 2018 (Agranov et al.; Toff). A contagem é minha, a partir da tabela.

**Definição e tipologia.**

- Definição (Introdução): "I define the bandwagon effect as a distinct phenomenon characterised by a positive individual-level change in vote choice or turnout decision towards a more popular or an increasingly popular candidate or party, motivated initially by this popularity."
- *Underdog* e voto estratégico (Introdução): "[...] the underdog effect, occurring when voters favour candidates performing badly in the polls, and strategic voting, where individuals vote for a candidate other than their preferred option in order to prevent an undesirable outcome. While the former can act as a countervailing force preventing the identification of bandwagon effects in aggregate data, the latter can be inaccurately mistaken for the bandwagon effect (Chung, Heo, and Moon 2018)."
- Tabela 1 ("A typology of bandwagon effects"), literal:

| | Static | Dynamic |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion | "Switching to vote for a candidate or party on the basis of their/its popularity at a given time, relative to the popularity of other options." | "Switching to vote for a candidate or party on the basis of their/its growth in popularity over time." |
| Mobilisation | "Turning out to vote for an already-preferred candidate or party on the basis of their/its popularity at a given time, relative to the popularity of other options." | "Turning out to vote for an already-preferred candidate or party on the basis of their/its growth in popularity over time." |

**O que conclui sobre efeitos.** O artigo não estima efeitos. O que diz sobre a evidência:

- **Onde a pesquisa se concentra** (seção "Bandwagon conversion and mobilisation effects"): "Of the reviewed articles, 59 have at least some conceptual or empirical focus on conversion while only ten discuss mobilisation (or turnout more generally) — and only three do so exclusively. Only one of these discusses mobilisation in dynamic terms (de Bock 1976)." E: "Only one political science study has empirically tested the possibility of static bandwagon mobilisation effects (Ansolabehere and Iyengar 1994)."
- **Estudos agregados** (seção "Defining the bandwagon effect"): "Perhaps unsurprisingly, aggregate-level study has historically found little evidence of an effect (e.g. Gallup and Rae 1940; Mendelsohn and Crespi 1970)."
- ***Underdog*** (mesma seção): "While evidence of so-called underdog effects seems to appear very infrequently (Hardmeier 2008), it is possible to imagine a scenario in which both effects occur in tandem and produce no evidence of an overall aggregate effect."
- **Comparecimento e efeito "Titanic"** (seção "Bandwagon conversion and mobilisation effects"): "The bandwagon effect is not to be confused, as this implies and was noted above, with what has been dubbed a 'Titanic effect': instances of individuals abandoning a 'sinking ship' by deciding not to turn out to vote because their party has no chance of winning (Irwin and Van Holsteyn 2000, 16–17)."
- **Experimentos** (seção "Being conservative in experimental study"): "experimental studies of the bandwagon effect seem likely to find effects when simply presenting polling information as the only stimulus and asking people to choose between the polled options. The insights from such work are limited, owing to a lack of experimental realism [...]. When this information is contextualised with other cues, for example party ID, there is evidence that the effect of the polling information itself is dampened (e.g. Fleitas 1971; Goidel and Shields 1994)."
- **Mecanismos** (seção "Static and dynamic bandwagon effects"): o mecanismo de gratificação "was explicitly discussed in twenty of the reviewed journal articles", e "More than one such mechanism has been assumed to underlie the bandwagon effect, including heuristic processing (entertained in twelve of the articles), contagion (three) and cognitive response (two) (Hardmeier 2008)". Em seguida: "Unfortunately, causal mechanisms have tended to take the form of assumptions rather than tested hypotheses in bandwagon research."

**Nota para o texto.** Na definição de Barnfield, a desmobilização de quem apoia o candidato que está atrás (efeito "Titanic") não é *bandwagon*, mesmo quando aumenta a parcela do líder. Nesta revisão, a queda de comparecimento entra como dano na célula `mobilizacao`. A distinção ajuda a explicar por que as duas coisas ficam em células separadas.

## 3. Moy e Rinke (2012)

**Referência.** Moy, Patricia, e Eike Mark Rinke. 2012. "Attitudinal and Behavioral Consequences of Published Opinion Polls." In *Opinion Polls and the Media: Reflecting and Shaping Public Opinion*, organizado por Christina Holtz-Bacha e Jesper Strömbäck, 225–245. Basingstoke: Palgrave Macmillan. doi:10.1057/9780230374959_11.

**Acesso.** O texto lido é o preprint dos autores no SocArXiv (doi:10.31235/osf.io/32z5j, arquivo "PollsMedia_2012_MoyRinke_PollConsequences_Preprint.pdf", depositado em 14/09/2018, "Pre-print version"). O registro no White Rose Research Online (`eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/143537/`) aponta para esse preprint. O arquivo foi lido com WebFetch. A ferramenta não converteu o PDF, e o texto foi extraído localmente, fora do projeto, só para a citação literal; a cópia foi apagada depois. O PDF não tem números de página impressos. Cito a página do PDF (p. 1 é a folha de rosto, e o texto começa na p. 2) e a seção. O preprint pode diferir do capítulo publicado.

**Tipo de revisão.** É uma revisão narrativa. O texto não descreve método de seleção, bases, número de estudos nem período coberto (buscas por "search", "database", "systematic" e "selected" sem resultado nesse sentido). Da Introdução, p. 2: "This chapter explores the attitudinal and behavioral consequences of public opinion polls portrayed in the media. We focus not on how policymakers respond to poll results, but on how the consumption of content from published polls can influence citizens."

**Tipologia.**

- Por desfecho, seção "Effects on voting", p. 5: "Today, voting-related research of poll effects can be broadly categorized by the criterion variable of interest – namely, whether the scholar is interested in the effects of published polls on turnout (i.e., whether to vote, a behavioral effect) or on voting preferences (the question of what or whom to vote for, which can be an attitudinal and/or behavioral effect)."
- Comparecimento, p. 6: "Effects of published polls on turnout among citizens can be classified simply as either mobilizing or demobilizing."
- Preferência de voto, p. 7: "Three types of poll effects on voting intention can be broadly distinguished from one another: the bandwagon effect mentioned above, sometimes also referred to as the contagion effect (Blais, Gidengil, & Nevitte, 2006); its converse, the underdog effect, in which polls lead people to support a trailing party, candidate or policy position; and strategic voting, which includes the possibility to vote for a viable 'second-best' solution if one's preferred candidate or party has little chance of success in the election or referendum."
- Efeitos fora da eleição, seção "Non-electoral effects", p. 9: "how published polls can foster political distrust, increase political engagement, and constrain opinion expression".
- Mecanismos, pp. 17 a 19, seguindo Mutz: informação sobre viabilidade usada de forma estratégica, heurística de consenso e resposta cognitiva. Na p. 19: "Mutz observes this mechanism [cognitive response] to be the most common among the three".

**O que conclui.**

- ***Bandwagon* × *underdog*, p. 8:** "Despite its breadth, the extant literature can hardly conclude whether it is bandwagon or underdog effects of polls that are generally more widespread and stronger. The inability to do so stems primarily from theoretical and operational inconsistencies: researchers not only study different contexts, but also emphasize different processes underlying these effects." E, sobre Hardmeier e Roth (2003), o trecho citado na seção 2. A seção fecha com: "At this point, we must note that more process-oriented, cumulative research rather than premature generalizations about relative frequencies and sizes of these two effect forms are in order."
- ***Underdog*, p. 8:** "Lavrakas, Holley, and Miller (1991) find, in their survey experiment, underdog effects of polls three times as large as bandwagon effects. Similarly, in her early review of the poll effects literature, Marsh (1984) concludes that there is a persistent suggestion of possible underdog effects but no support for the bandwagon hypothesis."
- **Comparecimento, pp. 6 e 7:**
  - "The literature provides evidence for both possible consequences."
  - Sobre a desmobilização por boca de urna: "Despite some evidence of such effects (e.g., Delli Carpini, 1984), a research summary by Sudman (1986) indicates that these are likely to be small and occur only if the polls show a clear winner of an election that previously has been shown to be close."
  - Sobre a mobilização: "a slightly larger body of work has produced findings showing the mobilizing effects of published opinion surveys".
  - Fecho: "However, to the extent that polls are received by citizens and shape their perception of election closeness, the available evidence suggests that they have an independent influence on the size of election turnouts."
- **Voto estratégico, p. 9:** "It is important to note that the promotion of strategic voting by published polls represents an influence on behavioral voting preferences, but not on the attitudes of citizens (Blais, Gidengil, & Nevitte, 2006; Forsythe, Myerson, Rietz, & Weber, 1993)."
- **Fecho, seção "Final thoughts", p. 21:** "It is important to note that poll effects are, by means of the mechanism underlying them, particularly contingent on the political and media context in which the information has been conveyed."

### 4.1 Coşgun (2026)

**Referência.** Coşgun, Melih. 2026. "Electoral Campaigns and Voter Behavior: A Systematic Literature Review and Agenda for Future Research." *Akademik Yaklaşımlar Dergisi / Journal of Academic Approaches* 17 (002): 1–20. doi:10.54688/ayd.1760361. Publicado em 25/02/2026. O autor é da Niğde Ömer Halisdemir Üniversitesi.

**Acesso.** Acesso aberto diamante, licença CC BY-NC (OpenAlex). O PDF da editora está no DergiPark (`dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/5132706`). Foi lido com WebFetch; como a ferramenta não converteu o PDF, o texto foi extraído localmente, fora do projeto, só para a citação literal, e a cópia foi apagada depois. As páginas citadas são as impressas (1 a 20).

**Tipo, número de estudos e período.** É uma revisão sistemática com fluxo PRISMA e o arcabouço *Theory-Context-Method* (TCM), sem meta-análise e sem avaliação de risco de viés descrita.

- Resumo, p. 1: "This systematic review applies the Theory-Context-Method (TCM) framework to 46 peer-reviewed studies published from 1996 to 2024".
- Base, p. 4: "High-quality sources were ensured by using the Web of Science (WoS) to collect the data."
- Período, p. 4: "The search was limited to articles published between 1996 and 2024 [...]. This stage yielded a total of 3825 articles."
- Idioma, p. 4: "615 papers that were written in languages other than English were thus disqualified."
- Resultado da seleção, p. 5: "After 2008 articles were eliminated because of this approach, 46 articles that concentrate on the connection between election campaigns and voter behavior remained."

**Tipologia.** Linhas teóricas, não tipos de efeito de pesquisa. No resumo, p. 1, são sete: "agenda-setting and priming, populist communication, negative campaigning, affective intelligence, poll/bandwagon effects, gendered mediation, and digital information processing". Na conclusão, p. 16, fala em "six theoretical paradigms", sem o processamento digital. A diferença de contagem está no próprio texto.

**O que conclui sobre pesquisas.** O texto não estima efeitos e não diz quantos dos 46 estudos tratam de pesquisas. O termo *underdog* não aparece.

- Seção 4.1.5, "Poll/Bandwagon Effects", p. 9: "The bandwagon theory posits that early leads in polling can trigger self-fulfilling voter momentum, provided the polls are perceived as credible (Rothschild & Malhotra, 2014). [...] The seminal U.S. time series remains the baseline for modelling poll shocks (Erikson & Wlezien, 1999). Still, conjoint evidence suggests that perceived methodological quality significantly influences whether polls persuade or polarize (Dawson, 2024)."
- Tabela de perspectivas teóricas, p. 8, linha "Poll/Bandwagon Effects" (colunas "Core premise", "Relevance to campaigns & voting" e "Representative studies"): "Lead indicators nudge strategic voters." / "Integrates polling cycles with enthusiasm/anger pathways." / "Erikson & Wlezien, 1999; Daoust & Mongrain, 2023; Dawson, 2024".
- Comparecimento, seção 4.1.4, p. 9: "Multi-wave Dutch-U.S. panels find that anxiety dampens, whereas enthusiasm accelerates, the bandwagon effect, turning emotion into the on/off switch for poll influence (Stolwijk et al., 2017). A Turkish lab experiment replicates the finding but shows that moral anger—rather than anxiety—drives turnout when polls are perceived as biased (Dawson, 2024)." Nas pp. 11 e 15, o mesmo experimento turco é atribuído a Neyazi e Kuru (2024): "moral anger can boost turnout when people doubt the credibility of polls, as seen in studies on Turkey (Neyazi & Kuru, 2024)" (p. 15). A atribuição é inconsistente no próprio texto.
- Método, p. 12: "Landmark laboratory work and survey-embedded experiments manipulating poll cues have delivered the field's strongest causal claims. Still, they remain skewed toward U.S. single-member-district contexts and convenience online panels."
- Regulação, p. 16: "standardized poll blackout windows—required in some areas—can help reduce the impact of emotionally charged bandwagon effects (Stolwijk et al., 2017; Neyazi & Kuru, 2024)."

**Sobreposição com esta revisão.** A sobreposição é parcial e pequena. A busca foi numa só base, só em inglês e só com artigos de periódico. A string trata de campanhas eleitorais, e não de pesquisas. Não há extração de efeitos, e a recomendação sobre janelas de proibição não vem de estudos de proibição.
